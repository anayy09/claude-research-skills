# Writing Quality

Standards for the COMPOSE step. The goal is prose that reads as if a capable
human researcher wrote it: direct, precise, and varied. Run the draft against
this before the AUDIT step.

## Contents

- AI-tell patterns to remove
- Sentence and paragraph rhythm
- Citations in prose

## The standard

Write to be understood, not to sound impressive. State findings plainly. Let the
evidence carry the weight; adjectives do not make a claim stronger. Precision
beats hedging, and specificity beats generality. If a sentence does not add
information, cut it.

## AI-tell patterns to remove

These patterns mark text as machine-generated and dull it. Scan for and remove
them.

- **Throat-clearing openers.** "In today's rapidly evolving landscape," "It is
  important to note that," "When it comes to X." Start with the substance.
- **Hollow intensifiers.** "very," "extremely," "significantly" (unless
  statistically significant and cited), "highly," "incredibly." They pad without
  informing.
- **Empty transitions.** "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally" stacked
  mechanically. Use a transition only when it marks a real logical turn.
- **The rule-of-three tic.** Compulsively listing three parallel items ("robust,
  scalable, and efficient") when one precise word would do.
- **Restating the question as a conclusion.** "In conclusion, X is a complex and
  multifaceted issue." Say what you actually concluded.
- **Hedge-everything.** "may potentially possibly suggest." Commit to what the
  evidence supports; reserve hedging for genuine uncertainty and be specific
  about it.
- **Summary bloat.** Sections that restate what was just said without adding
  anything. If a conclusion only echoes the findings, cut or sharpen it.

## Sentence and paragraph rhythm

Uniform sentence length is a machine signature. Vary it: a short declarative
after a longer complex sentence lands harder. Read a paragraph aloud in your
head; if every sentence has the same shape and length, break the pattern.
Paragraphs should vary too — not every one needs to be the same tidy four
sentences.

## Citations in prose

- Place the citation where the claim is, inline, as `[key]`. The reader should
  see the support at the point of the assertion.
- Prefer paraphrase to quotation. Put the finding in your own words and cite it.
  Quote only when the exact wording matters, and keep quotations short.
- Do not front-load a paragraph with a citation and then make several
  unsupported claims under its cover. Each factual claim carries its own
  citation.
- Do not cite in a way that displaces reading the source — a two-to-three
  sentence characterization of a source's argument is enough; do not reproduce
  its structure or a long run of its specifics.

## Quick self-check

Before AUDIT, reread the draft and ask:

- Does the first sentence of each section carry information, or clear its throat?
- Could any sentence be deleted without losing meaning? Delete it.
- Are the intensifiers earning their place, or padding?
- Does the sentence length vary, or is it a metronome?
- Is every factual claim cited at the point it is made?
- Does the conclusion say something the findings did not already say?
